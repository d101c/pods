# pods-spatial bench

19 Nov 2026, a laptop (i7-8565U, Ubuntu, PipeWire 1.6.8, WirePlumber 0.5.17,
libmysofa 1.3.5, KEMAR `MIT_KEMAR_normal_pinna.sofa`, 558 taps at 48 kHz).
CPU figures are % of **one** core. Raw output: `bench/results/summary.txt`
(not committed, regenerated by the commands at the end).

Since issue #26 both modes run the engine's renderer with diffuse-field
equalised HRIRs and the bass passed dry (`src/eq.rs`); Fixed is the same
renderer at a constant yaw of 0. The stock PipeWire `sofa` filter that Fixed
used before is no longer run; its figures below come from the first pass of
the same day and are kept for reference. "Raw" is the renderer with the HRIRs
as measured (`Tone::Raw`, `--tone raw`): it sounds exactly like the sofa
filter did (same HRIRs, same normalisation, levels matched to 0.01 dB in the
first pass).

## Safety

Everything ran in a **private** PipeWire, adapted from
`airpods-study/spatial-bench` (`bench/lib.sh`):

- own daemon (`core.name = pods-spatial-bench`) with no alsa / bluez / pulse /
  jack module and no device factory; two null sinks stand in for the hardware:
  `fake_airpods` (the target) and `fake_speakers` (must never get binaural audio);
- private WirePlumber with every hardware monitor disabled, under
  `dbus-run-session`; private HOME and runtime dir (`mktemp -d`, short path);
- `check_private` before every `pw-*` call and before every launch of the bench
  driver; the driver itself refuses to run unless `PIPEWIRE_REMOTE` is the bench
  core, and `Options::require_core_name` makes the library check the core name
  after connecting;
- `stop_all` at exit, then `ps` checked: nothing left.

The session's PipeWire, WirePlumber, AirPods and speakers were never touched.

One change from the study's bench: the private daemon loads `module-rt`
(RR 20 through RTKit, like the session daemon). Without it, its data loop ran
as a normal thread and both modes occasionally lost whole cycles (dropouts of
one quantum of zeros). With it: **0 dropouts** in every recording of both
passes.

## 1. Tone (issue #26)

The raw KEMAR set sounds "like a phone call": every one of its measurements
carries the measurement loudspeaker (no bass) and the dummy head's ear canal
(+12 dB around 2–4 kHz). The equalisation (`src/eq.rs`):

- diffuse field: power spectrum of all 710 measurements of the file, both
  ears, each weighted by the solid angle it covers (nearest measurement of
  8192 evenly spread points, nothing below −40° of elevation);
- 1/3-octave smoothing, inverted around its mean level (200 Hz–10 kHz),
  correction capped at **+12 / −15 dB** (KEMAR: the +12 dB cap is reached
  only above 17 kHz; +7.9 dB at 150 Hz; largest cut 12.8 dB at 2.3 kHz),
  held flat below 150 Hz and above 20 kHz;
- 512-tap **minimum-phase** filter (cepstrum), convolved into the 720 HRIRs;
  the common 21-sample lead (source distance) trimmed; HRIRs of 1024 taps,
  so the renderer keeps its 2048-point FFT;
- **bass dry**: below 150 Hz the stereo input goes straight to the ears,
  4th-order Linkwitz-Riley low-pass / high-pass (in phase, all-pass sum),
  the dry path delayed by 214 samples (4.5 ms) to stay in phase with the
  binaural band around the crossover (chosen at load time);
- one gain for the whole table (−1.7 dB) so the chain is as loud as the
  plain stereo output (BS.1770 K-weighted pink noise, mean of a centred and
  an uncorrelated signal).

It costs ~60 ms at start (on top of ~0.6 s of SOFA reading).

**Method** (`bench.sh tone azimuths`): pink noise is played into the sink and
one 4-channel recorder captures, in the same cycles, the sink monitor (input)
and the `fake_airpods` monitor (output). Uncorrelated L/R give the whole
2×2 transfer matrix (Welch, 8192 points); a centred signal (L = R) gives the
centred response directly. Everything is relative to the plain stereo
output (spatial Off, 0 dB):

- *speaker*: one input channel alone, power summed over both ears (Off puts
  it in one ear: 0 dB) — a virtual loudspeaker at ±30°;
- *centre*: L = R, each ear — the phantom centre between the two speakers;
- *azimuths*: the speaker response averaged over 12 head yaws (0°, 30°, …
  330°, Head Tracked mode), i.e. 24 azimuths of the horizontal plane.

Octave bands, dB re spatial Off:

| | 31.5 | 63 | 125 | 250 | 500 | 1k | 2k | 4k | 8k | 16k |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| speaker, raw (before) | −29.3 | −16.7 | −7.3 | −6.5 | −4.9 | −2.6 | **+13.2** | +8.8 | +1.1 | −2.2 |
| **speaker, equalised** | −0.1 | −0.4 | −1.0 | −0.7 | +0.4 | −1.4 | +0.7 | +1.3 | −2.5 | −0.4 |
| centre, raw (before) | −26.4 | −13.7 | −4.5 | −4.1 | −4.0 | −3.9 | +10.0 | +10.1 | +0.3 | −2.8 |
| **centre, equalised** | −0.2 | −0.4 | +0.4 | +1.8 | +1.4 | −2.4 | −3.1 | +2.3 | −3.5 | −0.7 |
| azimuths, raw (before) | −29.6 | −17.0 | −7.3 | −6.6 | −4.6 | −1.1 | +12.1 | +6.8 | +2.8 | −0.2 |
| **azimuths, equalised** | −0.0 | −0.5 | −1.3 | −0.7 | +0.6 | −0.0 | −0.4 | −1.1 | −1.9 | −0.3 |

Spread from 63 Hz to 8 kHz (octaves) and from 63 Hz to 12.5 kHz (1/3 octaves):

| | octaves, before | octaves, after | 1/3 oct., before | 1/3 oct., after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| speaker | −16.7 .. +13.2 | **−2.5 .. +1.3** | −13.8 .. +14.6 | −9.4 .. +2.5 |

- The target (±3 dB from 60 Hz to 12 kHz) is met for the virtual speaker and
  for the average over azimuths, and by octaves for the centre except the
  8 kHz octave (−3.5) and the 2 kHz one (−3.1).
- What remains outside ±3 dB in 1/3 octaves is direction, not colouring: the
  KEMAR pinna notch of the ±30° directions (8 kHz band: −9.4 speaker, −8.7
  centre; it averages out over azimuths, −2.5) and, for the centre only, the
  comb of the two speakers reaching each ear 0.26 ms apart (1.6 kHz band:
  −7.4), which real loudspeakers also make. The +1 to +2 dB at 250–500 Hz of
  the centre is the same effect below 1 kHz (the two speakers add in phase).
- The crossover leaves no dip: 100–200 Hz within −1.6..+1.7 dB in 1/3
  octaves for every response.
- Fixed and Head Tracked (yaw 0) give identical responses (same renderer).

**Loudness** vs spatial Off (BS.1770 K-weighted, both ears):

| | uncorrelated pink | centred pink |
|---|---|---|
| raw (before) | **+6.7 dB** | **+5.7 dB** |
| **equalised** | **−0.2 dB** | **−0.2 dB** |

Before, switching spatial on made the sound louder *and* duller: +13 dB of
2 kHz presence and 17 dB less bass. Level now matches spatial Off within
0.25 dB, so an A/B is not biased by level.

## 2. Transients when the yaw changes

Method of the study (`bench/analyze.py`): a 500 Hz sine (−12 dBFS) is played
into `spatial_airpods` and the `fake_airpods` monitor is recorded for 15 s.
Everything a linear filter produces stays at 500 Hz, so filter-switch
transients show as energy **above 2 kHz**, in 2 ms windows, in dB relative to
the signal. The yaw follows:

- `sine50`: ±45° at 0.5 Hz (141°/s at the peak), updated at **50 Hz**;
- `jump`: 0° / 60° alternating every 0.5 s (worst case).

Renderer: `Spatial::set_yaw` at 50 Hz, 1° dead zone inside the renderer.
Fixed: the same renderer, yaw never applied. The sofa rows are the first pass
(driver sending `spk_l/spk_r:Azimuth` with `set_param(Props)`).

| Quantum | Case | HF max | HF p99 | windows > −60 dB | > −40 dB |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1024 | Fixed (equalised, static) | −135.5 | −135.5 | 0 % | 0 % |
| 1024 | *sofa filter, sine 50 Hz (before)* | *−27.9* | *−29.2* | *48.4 %* | *9.7 %* |
| 1024 | tracked, raw, sine 50 Hz (666 changes) | −120.5 | −124.9 | 0 % | 0 % |
| 1024 | **tracked, equalised, sine 50 Hz** (672 changes) | **−121.6** | −125.8 | **0 %** | **0 %** |
| 1024 | tracked, equalised, jumps | −106.3 | −110.4 | 0 % | 0 % |
| 256 | Fixed (equalised, static) | −138.0 | −138.0 | 0 % | 0 % |
| 256 | *sofa filter, sine 50 Hz (before)* | *−16.0* | *−17.4* | *59.2 %* | *32.8 %* |
| 256 | **tracked, equalised, sine 50 Hz** (724 changes) | **−107.1** | −107.2 | **0 %** | **0 %** |
| 256 | tracked, equalised, jumps | −82.2 | −86.3 | 0 % | 0 % |

- The equalisation and the dry bass path change nothing to the click-free
  behaviour: nothing reaches −60 dB in any window, at either quantum, even
  with 60° jumps. The sofa filter clicked on every update (−28 dB at Q=1024,
  −16 dB at Q=256).
- 0 dropouts in every recording of this section.
- The unit tests `angle_change_crossfades_between_warm_convolvers` and
  `bass_path_is_dry_and_joins_the_binaural_band` check the renderer exactly
  (to 2·10⁻⁵ and 10⁻⁴).

## 3. CPU

Pink noise, 15 s, Q=1024 unless stated. Engine = the process that hosts the
renderer (the bench driver, i.e. what podsd does).

| Case | daemon | WirePlumber | engine | **total** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no spatial (player → AirPods) | 0.33 | 0 | – | **0.32** |
| Fixed (equalised) | 0.45 | 0 | 0.52 | **0.97** |
| Tracked, static | 0.46 | 0 | 0.50 | **0.95** |
| **Tracked, yaw at 50 Hz, equalised** | 0.45 | 0 | 0.70 | **1.15** |
| Tracked, yaw at 50 Hz, raw | 0.43 | 0 | 0.56 | **0.99** |
| *before: Fixed through the sofa filter* | | | | *0.67* |
| *before: sofa filter driven at 50 Hz* | | | | *4.34* |
| Q=256: Fixed / Tracked static / 50 Hz | | | | 2.44 / 2.21 / 2.54 |

Renderer process callback (measured inside it), Q=1024: 59 µs per cycle
static, 83 µs while turning, max 151 µs out of a 21.3 ms cycle; 0.27 % /
0.39 % of a core. Raw: 55 µs while turning. The dry path (8 f64 biquads per
sample) costs about 25 µs per cycle; the FFT size is unchanged. Q=256:
32–39 µs per cycle, 0.60–0.73 %.

Fixed now costs what Tracked costs when nothing moves (about 1 % of a core in
total, +0.3 point over the sofa filter, within the ±30 % noise of these
figures), and there is no `pipewire -c` client process any more.

Idle (nothing plays): the renderer runs **0 cycles** and every node is
suspended in both modes, so the AirPods can sleep (the filter is
`node.passive`).

## 4. Latency

One 4-channel recorder captures, in the same cycles, the sink monitor (input)
and the `fake_airpods` monitor (output); impulse once per second.

| Mode | onset | peak |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed and Tracked (equalised) | 15 samples (0.31 ms) | 24 (0.50 ms) |
| *before (sofa, raw tracked)* | *36 samples (0.75 ms)* | *52 (1.08 ms)* |

**No latency added**: input and output are in the same cycle. The trimmed
common lead of the KEMAR HRIRs (source at 1.4 m) brings the acoustic onset
from 0.75 to 0.31 ms. The minimum-phase correction adds none. The renderer
is zero-latency by construction (overlap-save over the shared history, every
call outputs the samples it received). The dry bass is delayed by 4.5 ms to
stay in phase with the binaural band, whose own group delay around 150 Hz is
the same (the measurement loudspeaker's bass roll-off left in the HRIRs).

Yaw to sound (outside Bluetooth): the yaw is read once per cycle, then one
cycle of crossfade, so a new angle is fully reached 21 to 43 ms after
`set_yaw` at Q=1024.

## 5. Supervisor in a real graph (38/38)

`bench.sh scenario`, both modes, with WirePlumber deciding the defaults:

- start: `spatial_airpods` becomes the configured and effective default; its
  output is linked to `fake_airpods` only; an application on the default output
  plays through it; `fake_airpods` gets −18 dB, `fake_speakers` silence;
- Listen Together (a `listen_together` sink appears and becomes the default):
  `Paused(Sharing)`, sink kept, default left alone; sharing over and default
  handed back: `Active` again;
- `stop()`: previous default (`fake_speakers`) restored, spatial nodes gone;
- the user picks another output: `Stopped(UserSwitched)`, their choice kept;
- the AirPods node disappears: `Stopped(TargetGone)`, default restored, nodes
  gone;
- tracked idle 3 s: 0 renderer cycles;
- no PipeWire error in any of these runs (issue #2: the AirPods node going
  away used to leave link proxies that were destroyed twice).

## 6. SinkWatch: is sound flowing to the AirPods? (6/6)

`bench.sh sinks` (issue #23): `SinkWatch` (the node monitor `podsd` uses to
start the head sensors only while sound plays) follows `fake_airpods` through
its `info` events: seen idle (`Stopped`), `Running` while `pw-play` streams to
it, `Stopped` again after the stream ends, `Running` on a second stream,
`Gone` when the node is destroyed; no other node reported.

## 7. Start and stop (issue #2) (9/9)

`bench.sh cycles` (`CYCLES=20` by default): 20 starts and stops in each mode,
pink noise playing into the spatial sink, the head turning in tracked mode.
Every stop is `Stopped(Requested)`, the default goes back to `fake_speakers`,
no spatial node is left, and no `PipeWire error … unknown resource N op:7` is
logged (before the fix: five per stop, one per link and one for the sink,
each proxy being destroyed twice).

## Limits

- Null sinks, not AirPods: no Bluetooth latency, no A2DP rate (the HRIR table
  is built for 48 kHz; at another graph rate the renderer still works but the
  HRIRs and the crossover are slightly off, `stats().rate` tells).
- The responses are measured at the renderer's output, not in an ear: the
  AirPods' own response (and their transparency/ANC processing) comes on
  top. Whether the result sounds natural is for listening, not for this bench.
- One HRTF (KEMAR, a dummy head): the elevation and front/back cues of a
  real listener differ; the diffuse-field equalisation only removes what all
  directions share.
- RTKit gives RR 20 to the bench daemon; the session daemon runs RR 88.
- CPU figures are noisy (laptop, turbo, the user's session running): about
  ±30 %. Orders of magnitude were stable over the passes.
- Elevation (pitch) is not rendered: the renderer has one HRIR per degree of
  azimuth at elevation 0.
